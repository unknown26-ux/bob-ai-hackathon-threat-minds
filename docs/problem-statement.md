# Problem Statement

## Background

Security Operations Centres (SOCs) are the front line of enterprise cyber defence. Analysts in these teams monitor incoming security alerts 24/7, triaging events from firewalls, intrusion detection systems, SIEM platforms, and threat-intelligence feeds. The volume of alerts generated by modern enterprise infrastructure is enormous — large organisations can receive hundreds of thousands of alerts per day.

## The Problem

SOC analysts spend the majority of their shift manually correlating alerts that should obviously belong together. A brute-force attack from a single external IP will generate dozens of individual authentication failure events across different tools, each appearing as a separate unrelated alert. A threat-intelligence match for that IP arrives in yet another feed. The analyst must manually recognise that all of these signals point to the same attack, calculate a risk level, look up MITRE ATT&CK techniques, and write a situation report — all under time pressure.

This manual process takes **2–4 hours per complex incident** and is error-prone. Real attacks are missed because analysts are buried under false positives. The most critical incidents are not always at the top of the queue. Alert fatigue is a documented cause of major breach failures.

## Who is Affected

- **Tier-1 and Tier-2 SOC analysts** who triage alerts daily
- **Security operations managers** who need fast situational awareness
- **Incident response commanders** who must make rapid resource allocation decisions
- **Any organisation** running a security operations function without a multi-million-dollar commercial SOAR platform

## Why It Matters

- The average dwell time of an attacker in an enterprise network is **11 days** — largely because alerts are not prioritised correctly
- Alert fatigue directly contributed to the Target (2013) and Capital One (2019) breaches, where automated alerts were ignored
- A 2023 IBM Cost of a Data Breach Report found that organisations using AI-assisted security tooling saved an average of **$1.76 million** per breach through faster detection

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

Commercial SOAR (Security Orchestration, Automation and Response) platforms like Splunk SOAR, IBM QRadar SOAR, and Palo Alto XSOAR solve this problem well — but cost $200,000–$500,000 per year and require dedicated engineering teams to configure and maintain. Smaller organisations, government agencies, and academic institutions cannot afford them. Open-source alternatives lack the correlation intelligence and explainability that analysts need to trust automated decisions.

ThreatLens demonstrates that a focused, transparent, explainable correlation pipeline can be built and run locally without external services, at near-zero cost.
